01 Ethics: Leave No Digital Trace

Technology ethics means using moral principles to decide how technology should be designed and used. Like respecting a campsite, ethical computing means thinking about how your actions affect others.

  • Privacy: Don't collect or share people's personal information unnecessarily.
  • Security: Build and use software in ways that protect accounts and data.
  • Fairness: Check whether an AI system treats people unfairly.
  • Credit: Respect creators' work and follow software licenses.
  • Accessibility: Make technology usable by people with different abilities.
Camp rule: Just because you can collect data or copy code doesn't mean you should. Ethical developers consider privacy, harm, fairness, and trust before releasing software.

02 Laws: Know the Trail Rules

Protect creative work

Copyright law

Protects qualifying original works, including software code. Copying or distributing code may require permission or compliance with its license.

Trail example: Don't take another camper's map and claim you made it. Likewise, don't present someone else's code as your own.

U.S. Copyright Office: Computer Programs

Protect kids online

COPPA

The Children's Online Privacy Protection Act applies to certain online services that collect personal information from children under 13. It includes requirements related to parental consent and data handling.

Trail example: A covered game for young children may need parental consent before collecting certain personal details.

FTC: Children's Privacy

Respect boundaries

Computer crime laws

Laws such as the federal Computer Fraud and Abuse Act address certain unauthorized access, fraud, and damage involving computers.

Trail example: Don't enter a locked campsite without permission. Similarly, don't break into computer systems you aren't authorized to access.

U.S. Department of Justice: Computer Crime

Keep the campsite safe

Privacy and consumer protection

Applicable laws may require companies to protect personal information, honor privacy promises, and avoid deceptive practices. Exact requirements depend on the product and situation.

Trail example: If an app promises to keep users' information private, its actual practices should match that promise.

FTC: Privacy and Security

03 The Developer's Trail

1. Plan the route

Identify the users, the data the software needs, and the laws that may apply.

2. Pack responsibly

Use permitted code and design for privacy, accessibility, fairness, and security.

3. Check the campsite

Test for bugs and security problems, explain data practices clearly, and maintain the software after release.
Remember: Ethics and law overlap, but they are not the same. Laws set enforceable requirements; ethics also asks what is fair, respectful, and responsible when the law doesn't give a complete answer.
